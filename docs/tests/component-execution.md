## Meter attempt and closure

Only a completed, authorised T-003 discovery record establishes the unambiguous instrument/configuration. The wrapper performs no separate discovery/help step, but every launched `spotread` child still uses Argyll's startup discovery path, which can probe serial ports (C-090). Before each attempt, recheck both Ex-Link ends remain disconnected, no other non-meter serial device/tool is involved, and the intended patch is held with menus exited. The owner records the current presenter's **frame sequence** from `pattern_buffer_applied.fields.sequence`, along with RGB and matching `pattern_present_intent` hash. Do not use the journal's top-level event number as the frame number. The plan's frame table supplies the expected sequence.

Bind `--presentation-log`, `--presentation-sequence` and `--rgb` to that current applied frame. The meter reads the receipt before and after the process. It rejects a changed, closed or incomplete presenter journal, but cannot detect every crash, overlay or physical display fault. Owner observation remains necessary throughout, including after a timeout. Each attempt needs its own pre-recorded purpose and identifier.

The proposed wait is `--wait-ms 2000`, freshly applied before **each** launch after confirmed menu exit and stable patch; `--timeout-ms 60000` is an exclusive acceptance deadline, not a promise of process termination. These remain owner inputs under Q-008. Use separate presenter and meter/discovery consoles. The latter retains T-003's clean, process-local environment for discovery and readings. The wrapper additionally removes both correction defaults from a copy of its child environment; that extra safeguard does not replace the console requirement. Its child uses exactly the plan's one-attempt argument list with no interactive stdin.

On a successful attempt, retain the final JSON, `meter_exit` and `meter_result` journal events, and after the CLI returns, the final `journal-close` with `complete: true`. The meter JSON has **no `closed` field**. Inspect `exit_code`, `capture_complete`, `log_complete`, `accepted`, `reason`, timeout/cancellation and dropped-byte fields together. A successful measurement requires a finite parsed result, exit code 0, complete capture/logs, no reported loss/failure and the plan's acceptance criteria. `journal-close` concerns the journal, not instrument safety or successful measurement. A result printed before a failed journal close is not a completed accepted record.

Timeout, cancellation, unexpected output or any other rejection ends the measurement schedule. The wrapper keeps draining and waiting for actual child exit and both output readers; do not advance or close the patch, start another attempt, kill/reset/replug, or treat the deadline as shutdown. Ctrl+C requests rejection in the wrapper and does not intentionally interrupt its separate child process; immediate delivery on this installation is untested. No further readings follow a rejected attempt even if the process later exits. Retain all partial/late evidence and the reason.

If no final result/return appears, process or capture completion is unconfirmed. If the presenter closes or changes meanwhile, record the interrupted patch and reject the attempt. There is no guaranteed finish time or automatic recovery. The owner can stop at any time under AGENTS.md section 7; force-closing or disconnecting may lose evidence or interrupt the driver and does not authorise continuation. The acceptable owner-directed interruption procedure for a persistently outstanding process, including console/cable handling and identification of wrapper versus child, is **unresolved and must be reviewed before measurement authorisation**. This preparation does not prescribe an unverified recovery.

**Process distinction (C-100):** `meter.py` starts `spotread.exe` with `CREATE_NEW_PROCESS_GROUP | CREATE_NO_WINDOW` and piped output. Microsoft's [Process Creation Flags](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/procthread/process-creation-flags) (S-049, consulted 27 October 2026) describes a new process group with Ctrl+C disabled and a console application without an assigned console window/handle. Claude Code (Anthropic) identified, and Codex (OpenAI) accepts as an **untested inference**, that closing the wrapper console may leave a windowless child running/holding the meter and lose later output. Wrapper exit therefore does not establish child exit. Ending the child is a different action that may abort the driver (C-069). The unresolved procedure must distinguish both; neither child persistence nor safe termination has been demonstrated on this installation.
